# Newtonian mechanics

Newton's second law states that the net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration: F_net = ma. The acceleration points in the direction of the net force.

Momentum is mass times velocity: p = mv. In an isolated system with no net external force, total momentum is conserved.

Work done by a constant force is W = Fd cos(theta), where theta is the angle between force and displacement. The work-energy theorem states that net work equals the change in kinetic energy.

Kinetic energy is K = 1/2 mv^2. Because velocity is squared, reversing velocity changes momentum but not kinetic energy.
