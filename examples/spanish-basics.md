# Spanish foundations

The verb ser describes identity, origin, profession, and inherent characteristics. The verb estar describes location and temporary states. Soy estudiante means I am a student, while estoy cansado means I am tired.

Spanish nouns have grammatical gender. Many nouns ending in o are masculine and many ending in a are feminine, but exceptions exist. Articles agree with gender and number: el libro, la mesa, los libros, and las mesas.

Regular present-tense ar verbs use the endings o, as, a, amos, and an. Hablar therefore becomes hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, and hablan. Regular er verbs use o, es, e, emos, and en; comer becomes como, comes, come, comemos, and comen.

Direct object pronouns replace the person or thing receiving an action. Lo and la mean him, it, or her in the singular; los and las are plural. The pronoun normally appears before a conjugated verb: Leo el libro becomes Lo leo.

Question words carry written accents when used interrogatively. Qué asks what, quién asks who, dónde asks where, cuándo asks when, por qué asks why, and cómo asks how. Porque without a space or accent introduces a reason: Estudio porque quiero aprender.
