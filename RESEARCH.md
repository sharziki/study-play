# Research → product rules

## What evidence supports

1. **Retrieval is the core mechanic.** Delayed retention improves more from testing than repeated study; retrieval can also improve transfer to new inference questions. The app asks before showing answers and generates both recall and transfer prompts.
   - Roediger & Karpicke (2006), [Test-Enhanced Learning](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)
   - Butler (2010), [Repeated Testing Produces Superior Transfer](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019902)

2. **Spacing depends on the desired retention horizon.** A synthesis of 317 experiments found that the best gap grows with the final retention interval. The MVP uses adaptive intervals; later, enough review history can justify FSRS.
   - Cepeda et al. (2006), [Distributed Practice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)
   - [FSRS reference implementation](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/free-spaced-repetition-scheduler)

3. **Interleaving matters, especially for choosing methods.** Mixed math practice improved delayed performance over blocked practice. Sessions therefore rotate topics instead of draining one topic at a time.
   - Rohrer & Taylor (2007), [The Shuffling of Mathematics Problems Improves Learning](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-007-9015-8)

4. **Gamification helps on average, but mechanics are not magic.** Meta-analyses report positive cognitive, motivational, and behavioral effects with substantial heterogeneity. Game fiction and social interaction can moderate results.
   - Sailer & Homner (2020), [The Gamification of Learning](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)
   - Kim & Castelli (2021), [Behavioral Change in Education](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8037535/)

5. **Points should communicate competence, not replace motivation.** In one controlled study, points, levels, and leaderboards raised output quantity but did not improve intrinsic motivation or perceived competence. Reward meaningful retrieval quality and calibration; skip global leaderboards.
   - Mekler et al. (2017), [Individual Gamification Elements](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2015.08.048)
   - Niemiec & Ryan (2009), [Self-Determination Theory in the Classroom](https://doi.org/10.1177/1477878509104318)

6. **Generated questions need grounding.** LLM questions can be useful, but fluent output is not proof of correctness. Every stored question includes an answer, explanation, topic, and verbatim source excerpt. The user can bury bad questions; future generation receives weak-topic and history context.
   - Kurdi et al. (2023), [How Useful Are Educational Questions Generated by LLMs?](https://aclanthology.org/2023.bea-1.47/)

7. **Useful pressure is challenge, not obstruction.** A study of 696 learners associated challenge stress with motivation and learning performance, while hindrance stress moved the other way. Boss rounds create an optional time target and bonus; lateness never removes earned progress.
   - LePine, LePine & Jackson (2004), [Challenge and Hindrance Stress](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ962030)

8. **Flow needs challenge near current skill and visible learning progress.** Challenge–skill balance predicts flow across studies; newer experimental work links accumulated learning progress to engagement and cognitive control. Difficulty should follow performance, and the UI should show mastery movement—not merely activity.
   - Fong, Zaleski & Leach (2015), [Challenge–Skill Balance Meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2014.967799)
   - Lu, van der Linden & Bakker (2024), [Learning Progress Guides Task Engagement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06592)

## Open-source pattern scan

- [Habitica](https://github.com/HabitRPG/habitica): XP, gold, levels, equipment, drops, and health make completed effort immediately consequential. Keep rewards and surprise; skip failure damage and social pressure.
- [Fluent](https://github.com/m98/fluent): Claude-native tutoring, one question at a time, immediate feedback, weak-pattern tracking, and a stated 60–70% success target. Generalize beyond language and keep state outside the model.
- [FSRS](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/free-spaced-repetition-scheduler): local memory state modeled as difficulty, stability, and retrievability. Adopt after enough real review history; the MVP interval rule is easier to audit while data is sparse.

## Product rules

- Reward: attempts, correct retrieval, honest confidence, and comeback after errors.
- Strong retrieval can trigger a small random shard drop. Surprise changes reward timing, never correctness or scheduling.
- Every fifth prompt is a boss round: visible time target, bonus for beating it, zero penalty for missing it.
- Never reward: time open, question volume alone, or easy-question farming.
- Streak is a session combo, not a daily hostage mechanic.
- Failure reveals precise feedback and reschedules quickly; no punitive animation.
- Clear typed-recall matches may be auto-scored locally; ambiguous paraphrases remain learner-graded.
- Bad content must be removable or editable in one action before it can damage trust.
- Confidence calibration and response pace may adjust mastery slightly, but never outweigh retrieval correctness.
- User keeps autonomy: choose material, quit anytime, bury bad questions.
- Difficulty targets edge of ability. Mastered items retire; saved time is the reward.
- Question mix: 60% weak/due, 20% new, 20% mastered keep-alive; rotate topics.
- LLM is content worker, not source of truth. SQLite + imported material own state.

## MVP hypothesis

If a session reaches the next hard question in under 10 seconds, gives useful feedback after every retrieval, and visibly converts honest struggle into progress, then study frequency and delayed recall should improve without requiring a fake economy.

Measure locally: session starts, completions, answer ratings, confidence calibration, response time, seven-day return rate, and delayed recall. Optimize delayed recall first; engagement only counts when recall does not fall.
